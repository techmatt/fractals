Full pipeline

The pipeline has three parts, and each of the pages before this one covered one of them. Finding good locations walks each fractal family and keeps the places the location judge thinks are likely to be good. Finding good wallpapers mines those places, drawing each one lots of ways across rendering modes and palettes and keeping the best few per place and mode. Gallery curation picks a whole gallery out of every picture that's ever been mined. In between sit two pools, one of locations and one of candidate pictures, and both only ever grow. This page is about how the three parts work together.

The loop

Almost all of the rendering time goes into mining. The solve is cheap by comparison (minutes rather than hours), so I re-run it whenever something changes, like a retrained judge, a new gallery size, or a color theme, and it always works over the whole candidate pool. Only the pictures that end up seated get rendered at full wallpaper resolution.

The loop between the parts is loose on purpose. I mine until the candidate pool looks healthy across palettes, modes, locations, and families, then solve at whatever size I want. If the gallery comes up short, or just looks thin, I mine longer and solve again. The solve makes this easy: for every mode floor it can't fill, it reports what was available and which rule turned it down, so it's clear what the next round of mining should go after. I don't try to compute an exact split of mining time from that.

What more work buys

When a gallery was short of one mode, I tried mining more of that mode. It did add seats in that mode, but it took them from other modes, because each location only gets one wallpaper, and the gallery as a whole ended up no fuller. What actually helps is having more places where every mode has been tried. So I mine every mode everywhere, and save targeted mining (one mode, one color, or one family) for targeted galleries.

The location search runs into a different problem. Once the walk finds one good frame, the easiest place to find the next one is right next to it, so left alone it would spend all its time in one valley. To avoid that, each run records the ground it covered, later runs push crowded neighborhoods further back in the queue, and part of every batch goes to ground where no run has found anything yet. That last part matters because the location judge learned its taste from what the search had already found, so it tends to underrate places that look unfamiliar.

Once there's enough in the pool to fill a gallery of a given size, more mining still improves it. I checked this by re-solving the pool at random fractions of its size: the seats fill early, but the quality of what's seated keeps going up at every doubling I measured.

Future work

The judges stand in for my eye. If someone retrained them on their own ratings, the same pools would give a different collection, without changing any of the machinery around them. That's the most direct way another artist could make this pipeline their own.

A location's framing is decided once, when it's found, and nothing afterward moves the camera. I don't know how much of a good picture comes from the place and how much from where the edges happen to fall, and it would be interesting to find out.

Palette and picture are chosen independently: a palette gets laid over the field, and nothing about it responds to the structure underneath. A coloring that took its cue from the field itself is the improvement I'd most like to see.

Everything here stays within double precision. Going below that is the subject of Deep zoom rendering, and Fractal atlases shows where in each family the locations actually ended up.
