Full pipeline

The pipeline has three parts, and each page before this one described one of them. Finding good locations walks each fractal family and keeps the places the location judge rates likely to be good. Finding good wallpapers mines those places, drawing each one many ways across rendering modes and palettes and keeping the best few per place and mode. Gallery curation solves for a whole gallery out of every picture ever mined. Between the parts sit two pools, one of locations and one of candidate pictures, and both only ever grow. This page is about how the three run together: not once, in order, but as a loop.

The loop

Mining is where nearly all the rendering time goes. The solve is cheap next to it (minutes against hours), so it is re-run whenever anything changes (a retrained judge, a new size, or a color theme), always over the whole candidate pool. Only the seated pictures are ever rendered at wallpaper resolution.

What ties the parts together is what the solve reports when it comes up short: for every mode floor it could not fill, what was available and which rule refused it. The policy built on that is simple on purpose. Mine until the candidate pool looks healthy across palettes, modes, locations, and families; solve at the size wanted; if the gallery comes up short, or looks thin to the eye, mine longer and solve again. There is no computed allocation of mining time.

What more work buys

The obvious fix for a short mode is to mine that mode. It does move seats into that mode, but out of others, because each location seats one wallpaper: a place seated in one short mode is a place no longer available to another, and the gallery's total shortfall does not move. What a gallery is really short of is places where the modes it lacks have been tried, and the way to get more of those is to try every mode everywhere. Targeted mining, at one mode, one color, or one family, is for a targeted gallery.

Search has the opposite problem. The neighborhood where the walk found one good frame is where the next one is cheapest to find, so a search that followed cost alone would spend itself on one valley. Every run records the ground it covered, later runs move crowded neighborhoods down the queue, and part of every batch goes to ground where no run has yet found anything. That reserve matters because the location judge learned its taste from what the search had already found, and it underrates unfamiliar ground.

Once a gallery of a given size can be filled, more mining still buys quality. Re-solving the pool at random fractions of itself shows it: seats stop being bought early, but seated quality keeps rising at every doubling measured.

Future work

The judges stand in for one person's eye. Retrain them on someone else's ratings and the same pools yield a different collection, with none of the machinery around them changed. That is the most direct way another artist could make this pipeline theirs.

A location's frame is decided once, when it is found, and nothing afterward moves the camera. How much of a good picture is the place, and how much is where the edges were drawn, is still open.

Palette and picture are chosen independently: a palette is laid over a field, and nothing about it responds to the structure it colors. A coloring that took its cue from the field is the improvement I would most like to see.

Everything here sits within double precision. What lies below it is the subject of Deep zoom rendering, and where in each family the found locations lie is the subject of Fractal atlases.
