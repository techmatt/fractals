Fractal atlases

Any interesting region of these fractals holds infinitely many small copies of the whole set, so a patient enough search could find a good picture almost anywhere. The atlas is not a map of where good pictures are possible. It is a map of where this project's search found them: every place that scored above the bar the gallery seats at, marked on the plane it came from with one mark to a neighborhood, one plate per plane.

The panel above is the fractal explorer's Atlas tab, running live. Blue marks are places on the parameter plane. Red marks are Julia sets, drawn at their value of c, which is a point on the same plane. On the Phoenix plate every mark is red, because its constants are fixed and every place on it is a view of that one set. Clicking a mark opens that place in the explorer, drawn as the wallpaper the search found there.

The atlas is fun to browse, and it is also useful. When descending into minibrots in search of pretty areas, it often helps to start at a place you like and zoom back out to the whole set, to see where it sits: which parts of the boundary tend to give graceful pictures, and which give chaotic ones. The atlas shows that for every place at once.
