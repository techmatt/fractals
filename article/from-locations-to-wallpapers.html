From locations to wallpapers

Finding good locations ends with a stock of admitted locations, and the sections since described the judge that rates a finished picture and the judge that chooses its palette. Two separate processes turn that stock into a gallery. A run executes for hours or days at a time: it searches each fractal family for highly ranked locations, and for every location that passes the location judge's quality bar it tries rendering modes and palettes and keeps the judged results. Runs accumulate; everything a run finds and judges joins a pool that no later run discards. The gallery pass is the second process: a single selection over the whole pool for a gallery of a chosen size, balancing quality against diversity across fractal families, locations, colors, and rendering styles, and only then rendering the chosen pictures at wallpaper resolution. Keeping the two apart matters because they want different things — a run wants to find and judge as much as it can in its time, and the gallery pass wants the best set, which is a property of the whole collection and not of any one run.

What a run adds to the pool

A run's first half is the walk from finding good locations; its second half takes the locations that walk admitted and produces judged renders of them. The input is the union of every walk ledger, each location scored once by the location judge. Within each partition — the buckets training judges introduced, one per family at one degree with a parameter plane counted apart from its Julia twin — the locations are ordered best first, and everything below the junk floor is dropped. Nothing in that list has been drawn beyond the walk's own thumbnail.

How much of a run's rendering time each partition receives comes from one table of intended shares (data/supply/release_mix.json). There is no right answer here; the table is my own preference for what mix of fractals makes the best collection. The Mandelbrot set and its Julia sets carry three times the weight of any supporting family, and the classic phoenix a small fraction. The same table steers the walk itself: it sets how much keeping-quality stock each partition is owed, and the clock goes to whichever partitions are furthest below what they are owed — where a find the location judge calls exceptional, by the third of its thresholds and the one read on P(≥4), counts ten times one it merely calls good (training judges, From a score to a decision).

Trying rendering modes and palettes on a location

Every location a run takes is rendered small several times, once per attempt. An attempt draws a rendering mode from one of the two kinds — either the plain smooth rendering, or one of the seventeen other modes. It dumps the location's smooth field once at 640 by 360, builds the 32-candidate palette neighborhood from color palettes, lets the palette judge pick one, and draws the picture that will be judged: that mode, that palette, at 640 by 360 with two-by-two supersampling. The render judge scores it, and that score — P(≥3) and P(≥4) — is what the candidate is worth from here on. Within a run each location gets one smooth attempt and two attempts in distinct modes from the other seventeen; an attempt costs a few seconds, so a few hundred fit inside an hour. Every candidate's row carries its location, its mode and settings, its palette, its kind, the judge that scored it, and the small picture itself, so a candidate can be re-scored by a retrained judge without being redrawn, and redrawn at any size from its row alone.

Choosing the gallery from the whole pool

The gallery pass takes a gallery size — fifty wallpapers, or a thousand — and chooses that many pictures from everything every run has pooled. It selects over the pool as it stands when the pass runs, and it is re-runnable: a retrained judge, a changed share, or a widened diversity radius produces a new gallery, and the previous one is simply a record.

Slots per fractal family

The gallery size is first divided among the partitions by the same table of shares the run used, so the question becomes concrete — fifty degree-3 Julia wallpapers, say. Within a partition the slots are split between the two kinds, with the larger share going to the seventeen modes — about three in five: I prefer a collection in which the plain smooth rendering is the minority. A partition whose supply runs out leaves its slots empty rather than borrowing from a richer one.

Geometric diversity

A desktop that cycles through wallpapers should show a diverse range of fractal families, locations within each family, colors, and rendering styles. Families and, to a degree, rendering modes are discrete choices that are easy to keep distinct. Locations and colors are subtle: two admitted locations can be nearby frames of one spiral, or frames of two different families that happen to look alike, and nothing in a location's coordinates says which. Color palettes defined an embedding for palettes — 32 OKLab samples along the ramp — so that distance between two palettes means what it looks like it means. The gallery pass needs the same thing for locations. Each admitted location above the junk floor is rendered once, small, in one fixed palette and passed through DINOv2, a vision network trained without labels to produce a vector that places visually similar images near one another; the vector is computed once per location and kept, and the leg that embedded the first twenty-five thousand took about thirty-five minutes.

Within a partition, the pass walks that space by farthest point: the first location chosen is the partition's strongest by the location judge's P(≥4); each next choice is the location farthest from everything already chosen, weighted by its own quality so that a lone oddity does not win on distance alone; and a hard radius refuses any location closer than that to one already taken. Walking by distance rather than clustering by density is deliberate. A neighborhood the walk has visited a thousand times is not a thousand wallpapers, and a method that follows density would hand it a thousand slots. The radius refuses a great many locations and has yet to run a partition short of picks, and the points a partition ends up choosing sit comfortably clear of it; the pairs that come nearest to it are pairs from different partitions, which the pass keeps apart anyway. A sheet of the closest chosen pairs is what that gets checked against, by eye.

Rendering many candidates for each chosen location

A location chosen for its geometry is not yet a picture. The pass takes the top few locations at each chosen point in the embedding — by location P(≥4) — and attempts each under several modes and palettes: more than a run affords, because the cost that matters is the wallpaper render at the end, not the seconds an attempt takes, and a location the walk spent hours finding deserves to be seen in more than one way. Those attempts are ordinary candidates, stamped with the pass that ordered them, and they join the pool like any others.

That leg turns out to be where a gallery actually comes from: nearly every wallpaper a pass seats is an attempt some gallery pass made rather than one a run had already made there. Of the two passes run so far, the first seated four of its forty-two off run-era candidates and the second two of its hundred. The attempts in a run are enough to establish that a place is worth something — they are how it earned its way into the pool at all — but a mode drawn at random and a palette picked once is not the same as looking for the picture. The gallery pass is where a location is looked at properly.

Floors, ranking, and the empty slot

Candidates are ranked per kind and per partition. The two kinds are never placed in one list. They are scored by the same judge on the same nominal scale, but they are rated separately and their pools are separate, and a shared scale does not make the numbers comparable: the same value can sit above one kind's bar and below the other's, and neither reading is wrong. Each bar is read off ratings directly, as training judges described — the score above which a picture had been rated at least good more often than not — and each is stamped with the exact judge it was measured against, so that a bar measured on one judge is never applied to another. In the gallery pass both bars remove: a candidate below its own kind's bar does not take a slot. Only the bar on the seventeen modes acts on what a run itself puts out, which is gated more loosely on purpose: a run is deciding how much of its own output is worth a look, where the pass is deciding what the collection ships. Above the bar, candidates are ordered by P(≥4), the judge's own answer to which of the good pictures are exceptional; P(≥3) has no resolution left at the top of the scale, and P(≥4) does. The interleaving across partitions is the one training judges described: every partition's best, then every second-best.

A slot is married to the point in the embedding that was chosen for it, and that is where slots go empty. The points chosen last are the most remote ones — that is what farthest-point selection means — and the most remote neighborhoods are the least likely to hold a picture over the bar. So a slot whose neighborhood yields nothing is re-seated: the pass moves it to the next farthest point and tries again, up to three neighborhoods in all. This is the one place worth being careful about what is being traded. Re-seating does not lower a bar or seat a picture the judge would have refused; it gives the slot another neighborhood, and a slot that has now failed in several distinct neighborhoods is a different statement from one that failed in a single unlucky one. Doing this filled a hundred-wallpaper gallery that had come up seven short at its first seating, without moving any bar. What remains after that stays empty. An empty slot is never padded with a picture the judge would not have kept, and it is also the most useful signal the gallery sends back: it names a partition where several separate neighborhoods had nothing to give, and that is where the next ratings and the next walk should go.

Rendering the gallery at full resolution

A seated candidate is rendered again at 2560 by 1440 with four-by-four supersampling, the same geometry for every partition, kind, and mode. Nothing from the attempt is reused — the field it was judged on is 640 by 360 — so the finished wallpaper is a fresh render from the location and recipe alone, and no judge looks at it. Every probability on a gallery record is a statement about the small picture.

Leveling happens inside that render. The picture is drawn through the plain palette and measured; if it falls outside the band color palettes described, the palette's lightness is re-baked and the picture drawn again. An acting row costs two full-size renders, and the operator acts on about four in ten of the renders it applies to. The direct traps and itinerary are excluded, as that section said.

These renders are the expensive step, and keeping them for the gallery alone is most of why the two processes are separate. With four in flight a wallpaper finishes roughly every half minute, and single renders have ranged from fifteen seconds to eighteen minutes. Every gallery row carries its location, its recipe, its palette choice, its scores, its leveling stamp, and the pass that chose it, so any wallpaper can be drawn again from its record alone.

How a run measured in days is paced, and what the pool looks like as runs accumulate, is the subject of running at scale.
