Making Fractal Wallpapers

This site is about rendering escape-time fractals quickly, searching the complex plane for the views worth rendering, and training small models to judge what comes back. The article is being written section by section. Completed sections are marked below; the rest show the outline. The code is in fractal-wallpapers, and finished wallpapers are in the galleries.

Contents

  1. Overview ✓

    The three parts the pipeline is organized around: finding locations worth drawing, drawing and judging many candidate wallpapers at each one, and curating a gallery out of everything that has accumulated.

  2. Escape-time fractals ✓

    The mathematics every picture on this site comes from: iterating a short formula on the complex plane, the escape time that comes out of it, the four families the project draws (Mandelbrot, the multibrots, Julia and Phoenix), and the coordinates that name one exact view inside one of them.

  3. Rendering fundamentals ✓

    Turning a grid of integer escape counts into an image: the smooth escape count that removes the banding, what the iteration cap and the sample count each decide, and fitting the palette's range to the frame rather than to the arithmetic.

  4. Rendering modes ✓

    The catalog of rendering modes: the recipes that reduce an orbit to a value other than its escape time. Covers the modes measured along the orbit's path, the orbit traps, the ways two renderings are blended into one, and the modes that paint directly while the iteration is still running.

  5. Finding good locations ✓

    The search for views worth rendering: what separates a good location from a mediocre one, the structural gates that reject most candidates cheaply, and the guided walk that descends toward the miniature copies of the set and reframes what it finds.

  6. Training judges ✓

    The three small networks the pipeline judges with: one scoring a location, one scoring a finished render, one picking a palette. Covers the four-point rating scale they are trained on, what each judge is shown, the network designs and training recipes, and how a score becomes a decision.

  7. Color palettes ✓

    Color as a step of its own, over a field that is already computed: where the project's nine hundred palettes came from, how one is applied to a stored field, why diversity has to be built into the library, and how a finished picture's tone is leveled against wallpapers that already work.

  8. Finding good wallpapers ✓

    At each location the pipeline has found, drawing many candidate wallpapers across rendering modes and palettes, and scoring each with a judge trained on my ratings.

  9. Gallery curation ✓

    Curation happens in two steps, and the second is the one that decides the gallery. A quality bar decides only what is eligible: within each rendering mode, a picture enters the pool if the render judge places it above a fixed threshold. Which thousand of those hang together is then decided over the whole set at once rather than picture by picture, under one seat per location, a distinctness test against everything already seated, ceilings that stop any one color, palette or rendering mode from dominating, and floors that ask each mode for a share. The bar outranks the floors: a mode with little above the threshold seats little, and some rendering modes do not appear in the gallery at all.

  10. Full pipeline ✓

    The three parts as one loop, and the two growing stores that connect them: what each part is for, how a gallery's shortfall tells the next mine what to look for, and where the pipeline could go next.

  11. Fractal atlases

    Where in each fractal family the pipeline has found locations worth a wallpaper, drawn as a map of the family with its finds marked, each mark opening at the view it stands for.

  12. Deep zoom

    Where double-precision arithmetic stops telling neighboring samples apart, what it takes to render right up against that wall, and what a picture drawn there can and cannot be trusted to say.
