Making Fractal Wallpapers

Rendering escape-time fractals fast, walking the plane for places worth rendering, and teaching small models to judge what comes back.

Placeholder page. The article is being written; the ten sections below are its outline and each has a page waiting behind it. The code is in fractal-wallpapers.

Contents

  1. Overview

    What this project makes, and why the hard part is not drawing a fractal but finding one worth drawing.

  2. Escape-time fractals

    The recurrences every picture here comes from — Mandelbrot, the multibrots, Julia, Phoenix — and what a set is when you can only ever sample it.

  3. Rendering fundamentals

    What a pixel costs, what the iteration cap decides, and the point in a zoom where double precision stops telling the truth.

  4. Rendering modes

    The escape count, smooth and discrete, and the frame-relative stretch that turns a field of numbers into a picture.

  5. Finding good locations

    Seeded walks that descend into the plane one rung at a time, the gates that admit a frame, and the record every candidate leaves behind.

  6. Training judges

    Collecting human taste at a scale a model can learn from, and the small heads trained on it: one for a place, two for a finished render, one for a palette.

  7. Color palettes

    Palettes as data, the tone band measured off wallpapers that already work, and a correction measured on the picture but applied to the palette.

  8. From locations to wallpapers

    Turning the union of every walk into a small set of finished pictures, and a durable account of why those and not the others.

  9. Running at scale

    Spending hours of compute where good material is scarcest: a census, a measured price per find, and an allocation that keeps its promises.

  10. Deep zoom

    What breaks once a view is narrower than double precision can address, and what it takes to keep descending past that.
