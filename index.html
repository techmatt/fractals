Making Fractal Wallpapers

Rendering escape-time fractals fast, walking the plane for places worth rendering, and teaching small models to judge what comes back.

Placeholder page. The article is being written; the sections below are its outline. The code is in fractal-wallpapers.

Contents

  1. The picture and the problem

    What separates a wallpaper from a screenshot of a fractal, and why finding one is a search problem rather than a rendering problem.

  2. The engine

    Escape-time fields in Rust: what a pixel costs, where precision runs out, and why every pixel is made in one place.

  3. Discovery

    Seeded walks that descend into the plane one rung at a time, the geometric gates that admit a frame, and the record every candidate leaves behind.

  4. Coloring

    Palettes as data, the tone band measured off wallpapers that already work, and the correction that is measured on the picture and applied on the palette.

  5. Labeling

    Collecting human taste at a scale a model can learn from: the rig, the append-only store, and the holdout that does not move when the corpus grows.

  6. The judges

    Four small heads — one for a place, two for a finished render, one for a palette choice — and the bar each was made to clear, written down beforehand.

  7. Supply

    Spending hours of compute where good material is scarcest: a census, a measured price per find, and an allocation that keeps its promises.

  8. Curation

    Turning the union of every walk into a small set of finished wallpapers, and a durable account of why those and not the others.

  9. The release

    What shipped, at full size, and how to re-derive any of it from the record it carries.
